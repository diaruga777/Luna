The bundled clip is a recut, not the source file. The source renders one frame
per hour of 2026; this takes the run from 14 Dec 2026 04:00 UT (a 22%-lit waxing
crescent) to the full moon of 24 Dec 2026 02:00 UT, every second hour — then
plays it backwards to its own first frame, so the loop has no seam and the moon
waxes and wanes forever. December's is the year's closest full moon (apparent
diameter 2009"), which is why that lunation and not another.

The recut is PORTRAIT, 540x960 (9:16): the source's 1280x720 frame is cropped to
the 720x720 square centred on the moon disc (the disc spans ~694px, centred at
x≈638, so the crop starts at x=278), scaled to 540x540, and padded top and
bottom with the black the sky already is. The lesson's mock post and the
extension-panel replica both show a portrait video, so the clip carries the
shape rather than being cropped at runtime.

The clip is used for factual/educational demonstration. No NASA endorsement is
stated or implied.

Reproduce with:
  curl -O https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/vis/a000000/a005500/a005587/phases_2026_plain_720p30.mp4
  ffmpeg -i phases_2026_plain_720p30.mp4 \
    -vf "select='between(n\,8332\,8570)*not(mod(n-8332\,2))'" -vsync 0 frames/f%04d.png
  # list.txt: frames forward, then reversed without the duplicated endpoints,
  # each with an explicit "duration 0.0333333" line (the concat demuxer
  # otherwise collapses same-timestamp images).
  ffmpeg -f concat -safe 0 -i list.txt \
    -vf "crop=720:720:278:0,scale=540:540:flags=lanczos,pad=540:960:0:210,fps=30" \
    -c:v libx264 -profile:v high -pix_fmt yuv420p -crf 26 -preset veryslow \
    -tune film -movflags +faststart -an LunaMoonPhases.mp4
