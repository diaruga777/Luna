Making time to read

A reading list is useful when it helps you find something you want to read. Save an article because it answers a question you have, tells a story you enjoy, or explains a subject you want to understand. Give yourself permission to remove articles that no longer interest you. The list should serve your curiosity.

Choose a time that fits your day. A short break can be enough for a short article, while a longer essay may need a quiet evening. Keep a note of the question that brought you to the piece. When you finish, write down the answer in your own words. This makes it easier to remember what you learned and to find it again.

Different articles reward different speeds. A familiar topic may be easy to skim, while an unfamiliar argument deserves a slower pace. Follow references when they help you check a claim, and stop when you have what you need. Reading is a way to learn and enjoy ideas; completing a queue is only one possible measure of progress.
